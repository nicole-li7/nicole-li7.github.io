InternScout

Internship hunting means checking dozens of career pages every day. InternScout does it for me. It pulls about 13,000 live postings from 1,700 companies, scores every one against my profile, and tells me what's new since I last looked.

how it works

Where the postings come from

Two community-maintained internship lists on GitHub are the starting point. With "Deep search" on, InternScout follows every link in them, works out which job board each company uses, and queries about 2,400 boards directly: Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, Workday, SmartRecruiters and Workable.

That turns roughly 4,000 postings from 800 companies into about 13,000 from 1,700, including roles nobody submitted to the lists.

The match score

Every posting gets up to 100 points: the right term (25), relevance to my major and interests (40), location (20), degree level (10) and how recently it was posted (10). Postings that want a different degree, or won't sponsor a visa when you need one, lose points.

Each score keeps its reasons, so the app can tell you why a posting ranked where it did.

Hiding what I can't apply to

A separate eligibility pass reads each description for hard requirements: citizenship or security clearance, no sponsorship, graduation window, degree level. Postings I don't qualify for are hidden by default, with a toggle to show everything.

Never losing an application

Saved and applied postings are copied into my own data, not just bookmarked by ID. If a company closes a posting or changes its ID, it stays on my Applied page marked as closed instead of quietly disappearing.

A real Mac app

The window is SDL2 + OpenGL + Dear ImGui, with dropdowns for university, degree and year and multi-select lists for interests and locations. It installs to /Applications as a self-contained bundle. A command-line version shares the same core library and data.

It checks for me

Leave it open and it re-checks every 15 to 120 minutes and sends a macOS notification when a good new match appears. Listings are cached for 6 hours so reopening the app is instant.

the tricky parts

Fast, but not too fast

Fetching one list at a time took about 36 seconds, and 8 threads brought that down to about 4. For the 2,400-board deep search I tried 32 threads, which doubled the failures because job boards started rate-limiting me. 16 threads plus one retry turned out to be the sweet spot, at a minute or two for a full refresh in the background.

Regex false positives

The eligibility rules are pattern matches over free-text job descriptions, and the first versions hid postings they shouldn't have. The word "undergraduate" once tripped a graduation-date rule. Now I test every new rule against a sample of real descriptions before trusting it.
